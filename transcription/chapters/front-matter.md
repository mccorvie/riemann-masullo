**Acknowledgements.** This work started off when the author was nearing the end of his first year as a Graduate Student at Stanford under Brian Conrad. It took nearly 12 years to assemble. Brian Conrad and Ravi Vakil were in the process of making arrangements for me to return to Stanford for a year to finish the polishing of the paper.

In light of the current climate I am being advised to release the current version as it is. The math is complete, and only filling in some writing remains on several parts across this long paper. The plan was to take a year of doing just this, with Brian Conrad helping me completing the task of polishing and improving the exposition.

The introduction itself is unfinished and will be soon replaced by a proper one, and the plan will continue as stated above.

I have included excerpts from a letter to Peter Sarnak that was sent to him mid October 2026, containing an overview of all the methods. Brian Conrad and I went visiting him at IAS last Sunday, for a four and a half hour long blackboard discussion with him.

Brian Conrad and I are aware of several glitches here and there that I was in the process of addressing on the way to completing the typewriting. The ideas are however all here, and many of the chapters are already fairly stable. The excerpts from the letter to Sarnak explain the overall strategy (generality: all number fields, all unitary Hecke characters).

If any message at all this work is meant to send to the mathematician who feels discouraged by the pace and tone of the current climate, it is this: we are still here. We are very much alive. We are not going anywhere.

Alessandro

**Notation and terminology**.

We denote by $\Pi$ the set of all prime numbers. We denote by $\mathbf Q$ the field of rational numbers.

We reserve the letter $F$ for finite field extensions of $\mathbf Q$, and denote by $[F:\mathbf Q]$ the degree of the extension. We refer to finite field extensions of $\mathbf Q$ as *number fields*. We denote by $\mathcal O_F$ the ring of integers of $F$, i.e. the normalization of $\mathbf Z$ in $F$, a finitely generated free (or “finite free”, for brevity) $\mathbf Z$-module of rank $[F:\mathbf Q]$.

We adopt the lexicon customarily used within Number Theory and refer to equivalence classes of absolute values (in the sense of [CF, Ch.II,§2]) on $F$ as “places”. We denote by $\Sigma_F$ the set of all non-trivial places $F$.

To a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of the ring of integers of $F$, we will associate the $\mathfrak p$-adic absolute value on $F$, denoted $\lvert\cdot\rvert_{\mathfrak p}$. In particular, to a prime number $p$ we will associate the $p$-adic absolute value.

For a non-trivial place of $F$, written “$v$”, we denote by $\lvert\cdot\rvert_v$ the corresponding normalized absolute value on $F$ (in the sense of [CF, Ch.II,p.51] if $v$ is finite, and induced by the Euclidean absolute value on $\mathbf R$, respectively the square of the Euclidean absolute value on $\mathbf C$, if $v$ is infinite).

We refer to the absolute values on $F$ corresponding to the prime ideals of $\mathcal O_F$ as the *finite places* of $F$, and denote by $\Sigma_{F,f}$ the set of all finite places of $F$.

We refer to the Archimedean absolute values as the *infinite places* of $F$, and denote by $S_\infty$ the (finite) set of all infinite places of $F$.

We reserve the letter $v$ to denote the arbitrary place of $F$, and write $F_v$ for the $v$-adic completion of $F$, by which we mean the completion of the field $F$ with respect to the topology induced by any absolute value corresponding to $v$: if $v$ corresponds to the prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, this is, up to isomorphism of topological fields, the $p$-adic field $F_{\mathfrak p}$, a finite extension of the field of $p$-adic rationals $\mathbf Q_p$, where $p$ is the residue characteristic of $F_{\mathfrak p}$. We write $v\mid p$ to mean that $F_v$ is an extension of $\mathbf Q_p$.

If $v$ is Archimedean, then $F_v$ is, up to isomorphism of topological fields, either the field $\mathbf R$ of real numbers or the field $\mathbf C$ of complex numbers. We call $v$ “real” or “complex”, accordingly.

For $v$ a finite place of $F$, we denote by $\mathcal O_{F_v}$ the subset defined by the condition $\{x\in F_v:\lvert x\rvert_v\le 1\}$, i.e. the unit ball within the metric space $F_v$. We denote by $\mathcal O_{F_v}^{\times}$ the “unit circle” within $F_v$, i.e. the subset defined by the condition $\{x\in F_v:\lvert x\rvert_v=1\}$. This is the unit group of $\mathcal O_{F_v}$.

We denote by $\mathbf A_F$ the adele ring of the number field $F$. We recall that $\mathbf A_F$ is the topological ring obtained as the restricted product

$$
\mathbf A_F=\widehat{\prod}_v(F_v,R_v)
$$

topologized with the restricted product topology, where $v$ varies over all the places of $F$, including the Archimedean places, and with $R_v=\mathcal O_{F_v}$ for every finite place $v$, and $R_v=\{0\}$ for every infinite place $v$.

We denote by $\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf A_F)$ the group of units of $\mathbf A_F$ topologized with the idelic topology, i.e. the topology induced by the anti-diagonal map $\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf A_F)\to\mathbf A_F\times\mathbf A_F$ given by $x\mapsto(x,x^{-1})$. We refer to $\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf A_F)$ as the *idele group* of the number field $F$.

We denote by

$$
\lvert\cdot\rvert_F:\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf A_F)\to\mathbf R_{>0}
$$

the *idelic norm*, defined on $\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf A_F)$ as $\prod_v\lvert\cdot\rvert_v$, where the product is indexed by all the places of $F$.

For a Hausdorff locally compact abelian group, we denote by $\widehat G$ the Pontryagin dual group, the Hausdorff locally compact abelian group of all continuous characters $\chi:G\to\mathrm U(1)$ endowed with the compact-open topology. We refer to $\widehat G$ as the “dual group”.
